# Mandolino

An offline, iPad-only coloring app for ages 4-10. It includes 264 original classic and animal coloring pages: 66 each in Easy, Medium, Hard, and Expert, from eleven designs per level (including a rabbit, ladybug, hedgehog, snail, elephant, bear, penguin mosaic, and bee mosaic), each in six versions with their own frame: plain, jewels, a petal wreath, sun rays, a double ring, and stars. Twenty pattern animals (dolphin, whale, frog, duck, mouse, dog, pig, sheep, octopus, seahorse, horse, giraffe, squirrel, koala, crab, unicorn, deer, panda, parrot, and dinosaur) have their bodies filled with a mandala pattern, a different one in each version; on Expert they sit on a round mandala background. Forty colors plus shiny gold and shiny silver, which paint with a metal sheen, a multi-color mixer with adjustable amounts, five glitter/crystal finishes with animated twinkles, a color eraser, and colored freestyle drawing on every page are included. Black pencil lines, circles, rectangles, and triangles remain available as outline tools. Expert pages contain at least 150 enclosed coloring spaces for a substantially harder challenge. A fifth tab, Art, holds twelve ornate pages drawn with a bold outer line and finer lines inside: six mandalas (lotus, paisley, feather, sun, heart, and lace) and six zentangle-style animals (wolf, owl, lion, butterfly, elephant, and peacock).  Make your own magic holds any number of own drawings: New drawing starts a blank page, and each saved drawing appears next to it, newest first (an empty new page is not kept, and a drawing cleared to nothing is removed). Delete this drawing, in the editor's … menu, removes an own drawing for good after asking. Gallery previews show the actual saved artwork, including free drawing and glitter textures, and refresh when you return from coloring.

German is the default language. Switch to English with the gallery's settings button or from the editor's … menu; the choice is saved. The gallery picks a level with four star-marked tabs and shows each design in its own row with all six versions side by side. The editor's tool tray (two side rails in landscape) holds Fill, Brush, Lines, and Eraser with labels, three size dots, a large Undo, sixteen everyday colors (including shiny gold and silver), up to six of your own mixed colors (press and hold one to remove it), and a button that opens all forty colors. In the color mixer, each tap on a color drops one part of it into the bowl (up to ten drops of each of up to six colors); the bowl shows the result live, minus and plus change a color's drops, and each color's share is shown as a percentage. Familiar primary combinations produce orange, green, and purple. Each mixed color you use is kept in My colors, newest first. Open Magic to choose no glitter, fine glitter, diamond, ruby, emerald, or amethyst from previews in your current color; the glitter you pick stays until you change it, whatever color you choose. Animated twinkles respect the iPad's Reduce Motion setting, and exported images retain the glitter/crystal texture.

Magic also offers solid color, gradients, stripes, dots, checkerboards, and chevrons, each shown as a preview. Use From and To to choose the two colors for a gradient or pattern; the arrow buttons make gradients run horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Fills stretch gradients across the selected region, while freestyle gradients use the whole page so consecutive brush segments join seamlessly. Patterns and gradients can be combined with glitter and remain visible in saved drawings, gallery previews, and exports. With Eraser, tap to clear a region or tap a drawn outline to erase locally; drag to rub out drawn lines, brush strokes, color, and glitter. The original mandala outlines are always preserved, and erasing supports undo/redo.

Select a color and tap inside a black outline to fill the whole connected space. The color reaches all the way into narrow corners where two lines meet, and specks of white too small to tap (like the middle of a tiny ring) take the color of the nearest space, so no white spots are left between the colors. Select Brush to draw freely with the selected color and finish on any page; changing colors keeps Brush active. Black outlines stay visible above painted strokes. Pinch the paper to zoom up to 4x for tiny details; use two fingers to move around the zoomed page. Under Lines, choose Pencil, Ruler (a straight line from where you start to where you lift), or a shape (circle, square, triangle, star, or a line star made of five crossing lines) and drag to add black outlines, then select a color to switch to Fill. Freehand outlines should be closed; gaps connect spaces. To help, a pencil line that ends close to where it started is closed, and one that stops just short of another line (or of itself) is joined to it, so a fill stays inside the shape. With mirror drawing, a line that stops short of the mirror line is joined to its own mirror copy, so half a petal and its reflection make one closed petal. Brush strokes are walls for fills too, so a shape drawn with the brush can be filled inside; tapping a brush stroke itself recolors just that stroke. Lines start exactly where the finger lands, and a line that turns into a pinch or two-finger move is taken back. Undo/redo supports the last twelve actions. Clear, in the … menu, asks for confirmation and is undoable. Short tips appear over the picture and fade away. A fill spreads out from her finger instead of appearing at once. Coloring pages show how many of their spaces are colored (for example 12 / 18); coloring the last one shows a message and confetti, and the gallery marks finished pages with a star (erasing a space takes it away again). The star is recorded even when she leaves the page right after the last fill, and pages finished before stars existed are checked once at launch and starred. Each page saves locally and automatically, including freestyle strokes and editable outlines. Existing saved drawings remain compatible. Brush masks cover only the touched area, coalesced touch samples render together, and outlines are cached. Gallery rendering and automatic file encoding run off the UI thread. Share exports the current drawing as an image using the iPad share sheet. No accounts, advertising, analytics, network requests, or third-party dependencies.

## Build and test

Requires Xcode with an iOS 17 or newer SDK. The checked-in Xcode project is ready to open. `project.yml` is its XcodeGen source; regenerate with `xcodegen generate` after changing target configuration.

```sh
xcodebuild -project Mandolino.xcodeproj -scheme Mandolino \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPad (A16)' \
  -derivedDataPath build test CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO
```

For a physical iPad, select your Apple Development team in Signing & Capabilities, pair/trust the device in Xcode, enable Developer Mode, and select it as the run destination. Wireless installation requires an active Xcode network pairing. Development profiles expire according to the Apple account type; this is not an App Store distribution.

Drawings are stored in the app's Documents/Drawings directory. Deleting the app deletes its local drawings; use Share to keep exported images.
